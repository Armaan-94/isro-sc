# A12. Data Interpretation (DI)

> **DI introduces no new mathematics.** It's percentages, ratios and averages applied to a table or chart. The marks are lost in **reading** the data wrong, not in the arithmetic. In the ISRO paper DI questions carry 2 marks, so slow down for 10 seconds to read labels carefully, then approximate.

---

## 1. A routine for every DI set

1. **Read the title, axes, units and legend** before looking at questions. (Thousands or lakhs? Percentage or absolute? Cumulative or per period?)
2. For each question, identify the **base** of any percentage freshly. It often changes between consecutive questions.
3. **Approximate** when the options are far apart.
4. Reuse computed values across questions in the same set.

---

## 2. Tables

Typical questions: % change, ratio, average, maximum/minimum growth, share of total.

**Fraction recognition beats long division:** 120 → 150 is +30/120 = 1/4 = **25%**. 180 → 210 is +30/180 = 1/6 = **16.67%**. Same absolute increase, smaller % growth because the base is bigger.

## 3. Bar graphs

- **Simple bars:** one value per category.
- **Grouped bars:** several values per category side by side.
- **Stacked bars:** segments on top of each other. A segment's value = **its top − the top of the segment below it**.

## 4. Pie charts

```
Value of a sector = (degrees/360) × total = (percent/100) × total
Degrees = percent × 3.6
```

- Comparing two sectors doesn't need the total: ratio of values = ratio of degrees = ratio of percentages.
- If some sectors are in degrees and others in %, convert everything to one unit first.

## 5. Line graphs

- The slope shows the rate of change; the **steepest** segment is the biggest change (not necessarily the highest point).
- If the graph is **cumulative**, a period's value = difference between consecutive points.

---

## 6. Speed tricks

- 1% of a number = shift the decimal 2 places; 10% = 1 place. Build other percentages from those.
- Compare fractions by cross-multiplying instead of dividing.
- Percentage share of a part = part/total; approximate the total if options allow.
- Average of numbers in an AP = (first + last)/2.

## 7. Practice set 1: table

**Car production (thousands)**

| Company | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | 40 | 50 | 45 | 60 | 75 |
| Q | 30 | 35 | 50 | 40 | 55 |
| R | 50 | 45 | 55 | 65 | 60 |
| **Total** | **120** | **130** | **150** | **165** | **190** |

**Q1.** Percentage increase in P's production from 2019 to 2023?
(a) 75% (b) 87.5% (c) 80% (d) 85%
**Answer: (b).** (75 − 40)/40 = 35/40.

**Q2.** Average annual production of Q over the five years?
(a) 40 (b) 42 (c) 44 (d) 45 thousand
**Answer: (b).** 210/5.

**Q3.** In which year did R have the largest share of total production?
(a) 2019 (b) 2021 (c) 2022 (d) 2023
**Answer: (a).** 50/120 = 41.7%; 2022: 65/165 = 39.4%; others lower.

**Q4.** Ratio of total production in 2020 to that in 2023?
(a) 13:19 (b) 12:19 (c) 13:17 (d) 2:3
**Answer: (a).**

**Q5.** In which year was the percentage growth of total production the highest?
(a) 2020 (b) 2021 (c) 2022 (d) 2023
**Answer: (b).** 2020: 8.3%; 2021: 20/130 = 15.4%; 2022: 10%; 2023: 25/165 = 15.2%.

**Q6.** Percentage decrease in Q's production from 2021 to 2022?
(a) 10% (b) 20% (c) 25% (d) 15%
**Answer: (b).** 10/50.

**Q7.** Difference between R's and P's total production over the five years?
(a) 5 thousand (b) 10 thousand (c) 15 thousand (d) 0
**Answer: (a).** R 275, P 270.

**Q8.** P's 2023 production as a percentage of total 2023 production (nearest)?
(a) 35% (b) 39% (c) 42% (d) 45%
**Answer: (b).** 75/190 ≈ 39.5%.

## 8. Practice set 2: pie chart

**A family's monthly budget of ₹60,000:** Food 25%, Rent 30%, Education 15%, Transport 10%, Savings 20%.

**Q9.** Central angle for Rent?
(a) 90° (b) 100° (c) 108° (d) 120°
**Answer: (c).** 30 × 3.6.

**Q10.** Amount spent on Education?
(a) ₹6000 (b) ₹9000 (c) ₹12,000 (d) ₹15,000
**Answer: (b).**

**Q11.** By how much do Savings exceed Transport?
(a) ₹3000 (b) ₹6000 (c) ₹9000 (d) ₹12,000
**Answer: (b).** 10% of 60,000.

**Q12.** Rent rises 20% and the extra comes out of Savings. New Savings as a % of income?
(a) 16% (b) 14% (c) 12% (d) 18%
**Answer: (b).** Rent 18,000 → 21,600 (+3600); Savings 12,000 → 8400 = 14%.

**Q13.** Ratio Food : Education?
(a) 5:3 (b) 3:5 (c) 5:2 (d) 25:12
**Answer: (a).**

**Q14.** A pie chart of company revenues shows D at 144°. If D's revenue is ₹400 crore, total revenue?
(a) ₹800 crore (b) ₹1000 crore (c) ₹1200 crore (d) ₹1440 crore
**Answer: (b).** 144° = 40%.

## 9. Practice set 3: bar graph (exports in ₹ crore)

2018: 120, 2019: 150, 2020: 135, 2021: 180, 2022: 210.

**Q15.** Average exports over the five years?
(a) 150 (b) 155 (c) 159 (d) 165
**Answer: (c).** 795/5.

**Q16.** In how many years were exports above the average?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
**Answer: (b).** 2021 and 2022.

**Q17.** Percentage decrease from 2019 to 2020?
(a) 10% (b) 11.1% (c) 15% (d) 9%
**Answer: (a).**

**Q18.** Exports in 2022 as a percentage of 2018?
(a) 150% (b) 165% (c) 175% (d) 180%
**Answer: (c).**

**Q19.** Which year saw the largest absolute increase over the previous year?
(a) 2019 (b) 2021 (c) 2022 (d) 2020
**Answer: (b).** +45 (135 → 180).

## 10. Practice set 4: reading traps

**Q20.** A cumulative line graph reads Jan 100, Feb 250, Mar 430. Units sold in March?
(a) 430 (b) 330 (c) 180 (d) 150
**Answer: (c).**

**Q21.** In a stacked bar, Boys reach 80 and Girls (stacked above) reach 100. Number of girls?
(a) 80 (b) 20 (c) 100 (d) 180
**Answer: (b).**

**Q22.** Revenue grew from ₹120 crore to ₹150 crore, then from ₹180 crore to ₹210 crore. Growth rates?
(a) 25% both (b) 25% then 16.67% (c) 20% then 25% (d) 30% then 20%
**Answer: (b).**

**Q23.** A pie chart shows A = 72° and B = 15%. Which is larger?
(a) A (b) B (c) equal (d) can't say
**Answer: (a).** 72° = 20% > 15%.

**Q24.** Sales were 200 units in year 1 and 250 in year 2. If year 3 grows by the same **percentage**, year 3 sales?
(a) 300 (b) 312.5 (c) 325 (d) 350
**Answer: (b).** 25% growth → 250 × 1.25.
