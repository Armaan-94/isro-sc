# C04. Statement-Based Reasoning (Conclusion, Argument, Assumption, Course of Action)

> Four question families that look alike but ask different things. The single most useful distinction: a **conclusion looks forward** (what the statement implies), an **assumption looks backward** (what the speaker silently took for granted before saying it).

---

## 1. The four types

| Type | Question | Judged by |
|---|---|---|
| Conclusion | What must follow from the statement? | Strict logic; no outside knowledge |
| Argument | Is this "Yes/No, because..." strong? | Relevance and practical importance |
| Assumption | What is taken for granted behind it? | Would the statement make sense without it? |
| Course of action | What should be done? | Feasible, proportionate, directly addresses the problem |

---

## 2. Conclusions

A conclusion must follow **directly** from the statement alone.

**Rules of thumb:**
1. Don't invent causes. "Teacher scolded Ram" ↛ "teacher dislikes Ram".
2. Advice or a direct implication related to the statement usually follows. "Petrol may become scarce" → "Petrol should be used carefully".
3. Don't convert hedges into certainties ("may", "likely" ↛ "always").
4. Ignore moral comfort; judge logical fit.
5. Watch for absolute words (all, only, never, always) that the statement didn't license.
6. A statement about **one** entity rarely supports a conclusion about **all others**.

**Examples:**
- "50% off on electronics till 31 January." I: no discount after 31 Jan (follows). II: no sales after 31 Jan (doesn't). → **Only I.**
- "Shyam is one of the probable toppers." I: Shyam will top. II: Shyam won't top. → **Either I or II.**
- "Recession made Company X fire 200 employees." I: X is known for firing. II: all competitors are affected. → **Neither.**
- "Only graduates can apply." I: non-graduates can't apply (follows). II: all graduates will apply (doesn't). → **Only I.**

## 3. Arguments

**Strong** argument: directly relevant, practical, addresses the core of the issue, states a real consequence.
**Weak** argument: irrelevant, a comparison with another country/person, emotional or vague, just restates the question, or relies on an extreme exaggeration.

- "Should smoking be banned in public places? Yes, it protects non-smokers from passive smoking." → **Strong.**
- "Should India raise its defence budget? No, neighbour X spends less." → **Weak** (foreign comparison isn't by itself a reason).
- "Should military training be compulsory? Yes, every child should do it." (restates) / "No, it's not compulsory in the USA." (foreign example) → **Neither strong.**
- "Should big industries be started in cities? Yes, more jobs. No, more pollution and crowding." → **Both strong.**

> Argument questions are the most subjective. Apply the checklist consistently.

## 4. Assumptions

**Test:** negate the assumption. If the statement now seems pointless or illogical, the assumption is implicit (valid).

**Signs of an invalid assumption:**
1. Absolute words: all, every, only, none, always.
2. Not connected to the statement's content.
3. About a different time (past/future) than the statement.
4. Contradicts the statement.
5. Scope mismatch (general statement, specific assumption or vice versa).

**Examples:**
- "Use liquid soap instead of soap bars." Assumption: bars are harsher on skin → **valid**.
- "Cellphones are prohibited beyond this point." Assumption: this warning isn't necessary → **invalid** (contradicts).
- "Smoking is injurious to health" on packets. Assumption: people read what's printed on packets → **valid** (otherwise the warning is pointless).
- "Electric vehicles pollute less." Assumption: EVs will be the only transport in future → **invalid** (only + future).
- An advert "Use our toothpaste for whiter teeth". I: People want whiter teeth (valid). II: Other toothpastes don't whiten (invalid; the ad doesn't need it).

## 5. Courses of action

A good course of action is **practical, proportionate and directly solves or reduces the problem**. Reject extreme, unfeasible or irrelevant steps.

- "Absenteeism among factory workers has risen." I: Shut the factory. II: Investigate causes and consider incentives. → **Only II.**
- "Road accidents up 40%." I: Ban all private vehicles. II: More traffic police and speed cameras at black spots. → **Only II.**
- "City water supply will be cut for 6 hours on Sunday for maintenance." I: Residents should store water on Saturday. II: Cancel the maintenance. → **Only I.**

## 6. Practice questions (with solutions)

**Q1.** "Ravi got the highest marks in his class this term." Conclusion: Ravi is the most intelligent student in the class.
(a) follows (b) doesn't follow (c) either-or (d) can't say
**Answer: (b).** One term's marks ≠ general intelligence.

**Q2.** "This medicine should be taken only after meals." Implicit assumption?
(a) It is safe on an empty stomach (b) Taking it on an empty stomach may harm or reduce its effect (c) All medicines should be taken after meals (d) It has no side effects
**Answer: (b).**

**Q3.** Advert: "If you love adventure, buy our shoes." Conclusion: Everyone who buys the shoes loves adventure.
(a) follows (b) doesn't follow (c) either-or (d) assumption, not conclusion
**Answer: (b).** "If P then Q" doesn't give the converse.

**Q4.** "Promotions will be based on performance, not seniority." Assumption: Seniority alone doesn't guarantee good performance.
(a) valid (b) invalid, uses "alone" (c) invalid, about the past (d) can't say
**Answer: (a).**

**Q5.** "Should smoking be banned in public places? Yes, it protects non-smokers from passive smoking."
(a) strong (b) weak, too general (c) weak, emotional (d) can't say
**Answer: (a).**

**Q6.** "Should India increase its defence budget? No, neighbouring country X has a much smaller budget."
(a) strong (b) weak (c) strong because factual (d) can't say
**Answer: (b).**

**Q7.** "Road accidents have increased by 40%." I: Ban all private vehicles. II: Increase traffic patrols and install speed cameras at accident-prone junctions.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** "School A has topped the district for three years in a row." Conclusion: School A will top next year.
(a) follows (b) doesn't follow (c) either-or (d) definitely false
**Answer: (b).** The past doesn't guarantee the future.

**Q9.** Cigarette warning "Smoking is injurious to health". Assumption: People read what is printed on cigarette packets.
(a) valid (b) invalid (c) about the future (d) can't say
**Answer: (a).**

**Q10.** "50% off on all electronic goods till 31 January." I: No discount after 31 January. II: No electronic goods will be sold after 31 January.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (a).**

**Q11.** "Shyam is one of the probable students to secure 1st rank." I: Shyam will secure 1st rank. II: Shyam will not secure 1st rank.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) either I or II (d) neither
**Answer: (c).**

**Q12.** "Due to recession, Company X fired 200 employees." I: X is well known for firing people. II: All competitors are also affected.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (d).**

**Q13.** "Liquid soap should be used instead of soap bars." Assumption: Soap bars are harsher on the skin.
(a) valid (b) invalid (c) contradicts (d) about the future
**Answer: (a).**

**Q14.** "Cellphone use is prohibited beyond this point." Assumption: The warning is not necessary.
(a) valid (b) invalid
**Answer: (b).**

**Q15.** "Should military training be compulsory? I: Yes, every child should go through it. II: No, it isn't compulsory in the USA."
(a) only I strong (b) only II strong (c) both strong (d) neither strong
**Answer: (d).**

**Q16.** "Should a disarmament policy be adopted? I: Yes, it promotes peace and encourages others to follow. II: No, it poses a security threat."
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both strong (d) neither
**Answer: (c).**

**Q17.** "Absenteeism among factory workers has risen sharply." I: Shut the factory immediately. II: Investigate causes and consider incentive schemes.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (b).**

**Q18.** Advert: "Use our toothpaste for whiter teeth." I: People want whiter teeth. II: Other toothpastes don't whiten teeth.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (a).**

**Q19.** "Water supply will be cut for 6 hours on Sunday for maintenance." I: Residents should store water on Saturday. II: The government should cancel the maintenance.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (a).**

**Q20.** "Should all schools teach coding from class 1? I: Yes, it builds logical thinking useful in a technology-driven economy. II: No, some children don't like computers."
(a) only I strong (b) only II strong (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (a).** II is a weak, minority-preference reason.

**Q21.** Which type "looks backward" at what the speaker took for granted?
(a) conclusion (b) assumption (c) argument (d) course of action
**Answer: (b).**

**Q22.** "Only graduates can apply for this post." I: Non-graduates cannot apply. II: All graduates will apply.
(a) only I (b) only II (c) both (d) neither
**Answer: (a).**

**Q23.** "Electric vehicles produce fewer air pollutants." Assumption: EVs will be the only means of transport in future.
(a) valid (b) invalid
**Answer: (b).**
