# 5.4 Process Synchronization: Critical Section and Semaphores

> 26 questions.

---

**Q1.** Critical-section requirement whose absence risks starvation but is often treated as the optional one:
(a) mutual exclusion (b) progress (c) bounded waiting (d) atomicity
**Answer: (c).**

**Q2.** Strict alternation with a turn variable fails:
(a) mutual exclusion (b) progress (c) both (d) none
**Answer: (b).**

**Q3.** Peterson's solution works for:
(a) any n (b) exactly 2 processes (c) 3 (d) only with interrupts disabled
**Answer: (b).** It satisfies all three requirements.

**Q4.** Counting semaphore initialised to 5 models:
(a) a binary lock (b) 5 identical resource instances (c) 5 waiting processes (d) 5 critical sections
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** In readers-priority readers-writers, wait(wrt) is called by:
(a) every reader (b) the first reader only (c) the last reader (d) no reader
**Answer: (b).** The last reader signals wrt.

**Q6.** Naive dining philosophers (everyone picks left first) fails because:
(a) violates ME (b) deadlock: all hold left and wait for right (c) memory (d) no semaphores
**Answer: (b).**

**Q7.** Block/wakeup semaphore with value −3 means:
(a) error (b) 3 processes are waiting (c) 3 resources free (d) 3 exited
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** Disabling interrupts fails on multiprocessors because:
(a) impossible (b) it only affects the local CPU (c) crash (d) it works
**Answer: (b).**

**Q9.** Test-and-set is useful because it:
(a) is fast (b) reads and sets a word atomically (c) disables interrupts (d) binary only
**Answer: (b).**

**Q10.** Producer-consumer uses E (empty slots) and F (full slots) plus a mutex:
(a) readers-writers (b) dining philosophers (c) producer-consumer (d) Peterson
**Answer: (c).**

**Q11.** Semaphore S = 10. Then 6 P (wait) and 4 V (signal) operations. Final S?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 0
**Answer: (b).** 10 − 6 + 4.

**Q12.** S = 7; 20 P and 15 V operations. Final S (block/wakeup)?
(a) 2 (b) −2 (c) 12 (d) 0
**Answer: (a).** 7 − 20 + 15.

**Q13.** Counting semaphore = 3; 5 processes call wait without any signal. How many block?
(a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 5
**Answer: (b).** Value ends at −2.

**Q14.** In the producer, swapping the order to wait(mutex) then wait(empty) can cause:
(a) nothing (b) deadlock when the buffer is full (c) faster code (d) starvation only
**Answer: (b).** The producer sleeps holding the mutex; the consumer can't enter to free a slot.

**Q15.** Shared x = 0; two processes each execute x = x + 1 (load, add, store) once, without synchronisation. Possible final values?
(a) only 2 (b) 1 or 2 (c) 0, 1, 2 (d) only 1
**Answer: (b).** A lost update gives 1.

**Q16.** Two threads each increment a shared counter (starting at 0) 5 times without synchronisation. Minimum possible final value?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 5 (d) 10
**Answer: (b).** A carefully interleaved schedule loses all but two increments.

**Q17.** A monitor guarantees:
(a) only one process active inside it at a time (b) no deadlock ever (c) no condition variables (d) busy waiting
**Answer: (a).** Condition variables (wait/signal) handle ordering.

**Q18.** A binary semaphore takes values:
(a) any integer (b) 0 and 1 (c) −1, 0, 1 (d) 0 to n
**Answer: (b).**

**Q19.** Spinlocks are appropriate when:
(a) critical sections are short and multiple CPUs exist (b) on a single CPU with long waits (c) never (d) only for I/O
**Answer: (a).**

**Q20.** Priority inversion:
(a) a high-priority task waits for a lock held by a low-priority task (b) priorities are reversed by aging (c) RR with priorities (d) deadlock
**Answer: (a).** Fix: priority inheritance.

**Q21.** In readers-priority readers-writers, who can starve?
(a) readers (b) writers (c) nobody (d) both always
**Answer: (b).**

**Q22.** A race condition is:
(a) output depending on the timing/interleaving of concurrent accesses (b) a deadlock (c) a fast process (d) a compile error
**Answer: (a).**

**Q23.** Fix for dining philosophers:
(a) allow at most 4 of 5 philosophers to sit (b) everyone picks left first (c) remove semaphores (d) busy waiting
**Answer: (a).** Or asymmetric pickup order.

**Q24.** Two processes: P does wait(S1); wait(S2); Q does wait(S2); wait(S1); both start at 1. Risk?
(a) none (b) deadlock (c) starvation only (d) race
**Answer: (b).** Acquire locks in a single global order.

**Q25.** Busy-waiting semaphores waste:
(a) memory (b) CPU cycles (c) disk (d) nothing
**Answer: (b).**

**Q26.** The three sections around a critical section are:
(a) entry, exit, remainder (b) start, end, loop (c) lock, wait, signal (d) fetch, decode, execute
**Answer: (a).**
