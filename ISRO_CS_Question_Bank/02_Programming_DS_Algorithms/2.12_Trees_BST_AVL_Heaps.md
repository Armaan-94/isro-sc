# 2.12 Trees: Binary Trees, BST, AVL, Heaps and K-ary Trees

**Notes:** [12 Trees BST AVL Heaps](../../PDSA_ISRO_Notes/12_Trees_BST_AVL_Heaps.md)

> 32 questions. Height convention: a single node has height 0 unless stated.

---

**Q1.** BST traversal that gives sorted order:
(a) preorder (b) inorder (c) postorder (d) level-order
**Answer: (b).**

**Q2.** Pair NOT sufficient to rebuild a general binary tree uniquely:
(a) pre + in (b) in + post (c) pre + post (d) level + in
**Answer: (c).** Works only if the tree is known to be full.

**Q3.** Distinct BSTs with 4 distinct keys?
(a) 4 (b) 14 (c) 24 (d) 42
**Answer: (b).** Catalan C₄ = 8!/(5! 4!).

**Q4.** Worst-case search in an unbalanced BST?
(a) O(1) (b) O(log n) (c) O(n) (d) O(n log n)
**Answer: (c).** Sorted insertions make it a chain.

**Q5.** AVL balance factor:
(a) h(right) − h(left) (b) h(left) − h(right) (c) h(left) + h(right) (d) depth − height
**Answer: (b).** Must be −1, 0 or +1.

**Q6.** RL imbalance is fixed by:
(a) single left rotation (b) single right rotation (c) right rotation on the child, then left rotation (d) left then right
**Answer: (c).**

**Q7.** Is 23, 17, 14, 7, 13, 10, 1, 5, 6, 12 (0-indexed array) a max-heap?
(a) no (b) yes (c) it's a min-heap (d) not complete
**Answer: (b).** Every parent ≥ its children.

**Q8.** Max-heap 25, 14, 16, 13, 10, 8, 12. Array after two delete-max operations?
(a) 14, 13, 12, 10, 8 (b) 14, 12, 13, 8, 10 (c) 14, 13, 8, 12, 10 (d) 14, 13, 12, 8, 10
**Answer: (d).** After the first: 16, 14, 12, 13, 10, 8. After the second: 14, 13, 12, 8, 10.

**Q9.** Full 3-ary tree with 41 leaves. Total nodes?
(a) 20 (b) 41 (c) 60 (d) 61
**Answer: (d).** L = I(k − 1) + 1 → I = 20; N = 61.

**Q10.** Full k-ary tree: 121 nodes, 30 internal. k?
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 6
**Answer: (b).** N = Ik + 1.

**Q11.** Parent of index i in a 0-indexed array heap:
(a) i/2 (b) (i − 1)/2 (c) 2i (d) (i + 1)/2
**Answer: (b).** Children at 2i + 1 and 2i + 2.

**Q12.** Threaded binary trees replace NULL links mainly to:
(a) save memory (b) allow traversal without recursion or a stack (c) make a BST (d) allow more children
**Answer: (b).**

**Q13.** Maximum nodes in a binary tree of height 4?
(a) 15 (b) 16 (c) 31 (d) 32
**Answer: (c).** 2ʰ⁺¹ − 1.

**Q14.** Minimum height of a binary tree with 100 nodes?
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 50
**Answer: (b).** ⌈log₂ 101⌉ − 1 = 7 − 1. (A height-6 tree holds up to 127 nodes.)

**Q15.** A binary tree has 10 nodes with two children. Leaves?
(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 11 (d) 20
**Answer: (c).** n₀ = n₂ + 1 in every binary tree.

**Q16.** A full binary tree with 20 leaves has how many nodes?
(a) 39 (b) 40 (c) 41 (d) 19
**Answer: (a).** 19 internal + 20 leaves.

**Q17.** Postorder of the BST with preorder 50 30 20 40 70 60 80?
(a) 20 40 30 60 80 70 50 (b) 20 30 40 50 60 70 80 (c) 50 30 70 20 40 60 80 (d) 40 20 30 80 60 70 50
**Answer: (a).**

**Q18.** Level-order of the BST in Q17?
(a) 50 30 70 20 40 60 80 (b) 50 30 20 40 70 60 80 (c) 20 30 40 50 60 70 80 (d) 50 70 30 80 60 40 20
**Answer: (a).**

**Q19.** BST with levels: 10 (level 1); 5, 20 (2); 4, 15, 30 (3); 11 (4). Average comparisons for a successful search?
(a) 2 (b) 18/7 (c) 3 (d) 16/7
**Answer: (b).** (1 + 4 + 9 + 4)/7.

**Q20.** Insert 1, 2, 3 into an empty AVL tree. Root?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) unbalanced
**Answer: (b).** RR case → one left rotation.

**Q21.** Minimum nodes in an AVL tree of height 4?
(a) 7 (b) 12 (c) 15 (d) 20
**Answer: (b).** N(h) = N(h − 1) + N(h − 2) + 1 with N(0) = 1, N(1) = 2: 4, 7, 12.

**Q22.** Maximum height of an AVL tree with 7 nodes?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6
**Answer: (b).** N(3) = 7, so 7 nodes can reach height 3 but not 4 (needs 12).

**Q23.** Building a heap from n elements bottom-up costs:
(a) O(n) (b) O(n log n) (c) O(log n) (d) O(n²)
**Answer: (a).** Inserting one by one would be O(n log n).

**Q24.** In a min-heap, the largest element is:
(a) at the root (b) at some leaf (c) at index 1 (d) the last element always
**Answer: (b).** Any internal node has a child larger than itself, so the maximum can't be internal.

**Q25.** Insert 50 into max-heap [40, 30, 20, 10, 15]. Resulting array?
(a) [50, 30, 40, 10, 15, 20] (b) [50, 40, 30, 10, 15, 20] (c) [40, 30, 50, 10, 15, 20] (d) [50, 30, 20, 10, 15, 40]
**Answer: (a).** 50 goes to index 5, swaps with 20 (index 2), then with 40 (index 0).

**Q26.** Distinct unlabelled binary trees with 3 nodes?
(a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 30
**Answer: (b).** With 3 distinct labels: 5 × 3! = 30.

**Q27.** Height of a complete binary tree with n nodes:
(a) ⌊log₂ n⌋ (b) n − 1 (c) √n (d) n/2
**Answer: (a).**

**Q28.** The inorder successor of a BST node that has a right subtree is:
(a) its parent (b) the leftmost node of its right subtree (c) the rightmost of its left subtree (d) the root
**Answer: (b).** It has no left child, so deleting it is easy.

**Q29.** Full 5-ary tree with 26 internal nodes. Leaves?
(a) 104 (b) 105 (c) 130 (d) 131
**Answer: (b).** 26 × 4 + 1.

**Q30.** Is every heap a BST?
(a) yes (b) no, a heap only orders parent and child (c) only min-heaps (d) only complete heaps
**Answer: (b).**

**Q31.** Traversal used to delete a whole tree safely:
(a) preorder (b) inorder (c) postorder (d) level-order
**Answer: (c).** Children are freed before the parent.

**Q32.** Insert (or delete) in an AVL tree takes in the worst case:
(a) O(1) (b) O(log n) (c) O(n) (d) O(n log n)
**Answer: (b).**
