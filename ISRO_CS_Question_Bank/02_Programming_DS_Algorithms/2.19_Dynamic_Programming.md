# 2.19 Dynamic Programming

> 24 questions. DP = overlapping subproblems + optimal substructure; solve each subproblem once and store it.

---

**Q1.** What makes DP applicable but not plain divide and conquer?
(a) recursion (b) overlapping subproblems (c) sorting (d) graphs only
**Answer: (b).** Merge sort's subproblems never repeat, so caching gains nothing.

**Q2.** LCS length of "ABCBDAB" and "BDCABA"?
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 6
**Answer: (b).** e.g. BCBA or BDAB.

**Q3.** A1 30×35, A2 35×15, A3 15×5, A4 5×10, A5 10×20. Minimum scalar multiplications?
(a) 30000 (b) 15125 (c) 7875 (d) 45500
**Answer: (b).** ((A1(A2A3))(A4A5)).

**Q4.** M1 10×100, M2 100×20, M3 20×5, M4 5×80. Optimal cost and grouping?
(a) 44000, (M1M2)(M3M4) (b) 25000, ((M1M2)M3)M4 (c) 19000, (M1(M2M3))M4 (d) all equal
**Answer: (c).** M2M3 costs 10000 (result 100×5); M1 times that costs 5000 (result 10×5); times M4 costs 4000. Total 19000.

**Q5.** MCM DP time for n matrices:
(a) O(n) (b) O(n log n) (c) O(n²) (d) O(n³)
**Answer: (d).**

**Q6.** Floyd-Warshall gives meaningless results with:
(a) negative edges (b) a negative cycle (c) disconnected graphs (d) zero self-loops
**Answer: (b).**

**Q7.** Does {2, 3, 7, 8, 10} have a subset summing to 14?
(a) yes (b) no (c) can't say (d) two of them
**Answer: (b).** Achievable sums near 14 are 13 and 15.

**Q8.** Memoisation vs tabulation:
(a) different complexity (b) tabulation is iterative bottom-up with no recursion overhead (c) only memoisation is correct (d) tabulation can't rebuild the solution
**Answer: (b).**

**Q9.** LCS length of "ABCD" and "ACBD"?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 1
**Answer: (b).** ABD or ACD.

**Q10.** LCS of "AGGTAB" and "GXTXAYB"?
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 2
**Answer: (b).** GTAB.

**Q11.** LCS DP table time for strings of lengths m and n:
(a) O(m + n) (b) O(mn) (c) O(2ᵐ) (d) O(m log n)
**Answer: (b).** Brute force over all subsequences is exponential.

**Q12.** A 10×20, B 20×30, C 30×40. Minimum multiplications?
(a) 18000 (b) 32000 (c) 24000 (d) 26000
**Answer: (a).** (AB)C = 6000 + 12000; A(BC) = 24000 + 8000.

**Q13.** Number of ways to parenthesise a chain of 4 matrices?
(a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 14
**Answer: (b).** Catalan number C₃.

**Q14.** 0/1 knapsack, W = 5; items (weight, value): (1, 1), (3, 4), (4, 5), (5, 7). Maximum value?
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 9
**Answer: (b).** (5, 7) alone beats (4, 5) + (1, 1) = 6 and (3, 4) + (1, 1) = 5.

**Q15.** 0/1 knapsack DP runs in O(nW). This is called:
(a) polynomial (b) pseudo-polynomial (c) exponential always (d) logarithmic
**Answer: (b).** It's polynomial in the value of W, not in its bit length. The problem is NP-complete.

**Q16.** Edit distance between "kitten" and "sitting"?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5
**Answer: (b).** k→s, e→i, insert g.

**Q17.** Number of ways to make 4 with coins {1, 2, 3} (order doesn't matter)?
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 7
**Answer: (b).** 1111, 112, 22, 13.

**Q18.** Longest increasing subsequence of 10, 9, 2, 5, 3, 7, 101, 18?
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 6
**Answer: (b).** e.g. 2, 3, 7, 18.

**Q19.** Fibonacci with DP takes:
(a) O(2ⁿ) (b) O(n) (c) O(n²) (d) O(log n) only
**Answer: (b).** Naive recursion is exponential.

**Q20.** Floyd-Warshall time:
(a) O(V²) (b) O(V³) (c) O(VE) (d) O(E log V)
**Answer: (b).**

**Q21.** Floyd-Warshall on 1→2 = 5, 2→3 = 2, 3→1 = 1. Shortest distance from 2 to 1?
(a) ∞ (b) 3 (c) 6 (d) 8
**Answer: (b).** 2→3→1.

**Q22.** Same graph, 3 to 2?
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) ∞
**Answer: (b).** 3→1→2.

**Q23.** DP problems among these:
(a) LCS, MCM, 0/1 knapsack, Floyd-Warshall, Bellman-Ford (b) Prim, Kruskal (c) Huffman (d) binary search
**Answer: (a).**

**Q24.** The four DP steps end with:
(a) sorting the input (b) reconstructing the optimal solution from the table (c) a greedy pass (d) hashing
**Answer: (b).**
