# 1.8 Pipelining and Instruction-Level Parallelism

> 28 questions with speedup, stall and CPI numericals.

---

**Q1.** Pipelining primarily improves:
(a) latency of one instruction (b) throughput (c) clock speed (d) instruction-set size
**Answer: (b).**

**Q2.** A structural hazard arises when:
(a) a result isn't ready (b) two instructions need the same hardware at once (c) a branch target is unknown (d) there are too many stages
**Answer: (b).** Fix: duplicate the resource (e.g., separate I-cache and D-cache).

**Q3.** The true data dependency that occurs even in simple in-order pipelines:
(a) WAR (b) WAW (c) RAW (d) none
**Answer: (c).**

**Q4.** Forwarding works by:
(a) inserting bubbles (b) routing a result straight from a later stage to the stage that needs it (c) predicting branches (d) duplicating the ALU
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** With perfect forwarding, a load-use hazard still needs:
(a) no stall (b) at least one stall (c) a full flush (d) disabling the pipeline
**Answer: (b).** The loaded value only exists after MEM.

**Q6.** A control hazard arises because:
(a) two instructions want one unit (b) the next instruction is unknown until the branch resolves (c) a register is written too fast (d) clock too high
**Answer: (b).**

**Q7.** Branch misprediction penalty is worse in:
(a) shorter pipelines (b) deeper pipelines (c) pipelines without branches (d) structural-only pipelines
**Answer: (b).** More wrongly fetched instructions to flush.

**Q8.** Ideal 5-stage pipeline, 100 instructions. Cycles?
(a) 500 (b) 100 (c) 104 (d) 20
**Answer: (c).** k + n − 1.

**Q9.** Static branch prediction:
(a) uses runtime history (b) uses a fixed rule decided in advance (c) always stalls (d) is 100% accurate
**Answer: (b).**

**Q10.** WAR and WAW hazards matter only when:
(a) instructions complete in order (b) instructions can complete out of order (c) only structural hazards exist (d) forwarding is off
**Answer: (b).** Register renaming removes them.

**Q11.** 4-stage pipeline, 100 instructions, equal stages. Speedup over non-pipelined?
(a) 4 (b) 3.88 (c) 3.5 (d) 2.5
**Answer: (b).** 400/103.

**Q12.** Stage delays 10, 20, 15, 25 ns; latch delay 5 ns. Pipeline cycle time?
(a) 25 ns (b) 30 ns (c) 17.5 ns (d) 75 ns
**Answer: (b).** Slowest stage + latch.

**Q13.** For Q12, 1000 instructions. Speedup over a non-pipelined unit that takes 70 ns per instruction?
(a) 2.33 (b) 2.8 (c) 3.5 (d) 4
**Answer: (a).** Pipelined: (4 + 999) × 30 = 30,090 ns; non-pipelined 70,000 ns.

**Q14.** Maximum (ideal) speedup of a k-stage pipeline for very many instructions:
(a) k (b) 2k (c) k − 1 (d) log k
**Answer: (a).**

**Q15.** Pipeline efficiency is:
(a) speedup/k (b) k/speedup (c) speedup × k (d) 1/k
**Answer: (a).**

**Q16.** Cycle time 30 ns, CPI 1. Throughput?
(a) 30 MIPS (b) 33.3 MIPS (c) 3.3 MIPS (d) 300 MIPS
**Answer: (b).** 1/30 ns.

**Q17.** 20% of instructions are branches with a 3-cycle penalty. Effective CPI (base 1)?
(a) 1.2 (b) 1.6 (c) 3.0 (d) 1.3
**Answer: (b).** 1 + 0.2 × 3.

**Q18.** 25% loads; 40% of loads are immediately followed by a dependent instruction (1 stall each). CPI?
(a) 1.1 (b) 1.25 (c) 1.4 (d) 1.04
**Answer: (a).** 1 + 0.25 × 0.4 × 1.

**Q19.** In delayed branching, the instruction in the delay slot:
(a) is always discarded (b) always executes, whether or not the branch is taken (c) executes only if taken (d) is a NOP by hardware
**Answer: (b).** The compiler fills it with useful work when possible.

**Q20.** A superscalar processor:
(a) issues multiple instructions per cycle (CPI can drop below 1) (b) has one very deep pipeline (c) has no pipeline (d) relies only on the compiler for parallelism
**Answer: (a).**

**Q21.** I1: R1 ← R2 + R3; I2: R4 ← R1 + R5. Hazard?
(a) RAW on R1 (b) WAR on R1 (c) WAW on R1 (d) none
**Answer: (a).**

**Q22.** I1: R4 ← R1 + R2; I2: R1 ← R5 + R6. Hazard?
(a) RAW (b) WAR on R1 (c) WAW (d) structural
**Answer: (b).** I2 writes R1 after I1 reads it.

**Q23.** I1: R1 ← R2 + R3; I2: R1 ← R4 + R5. Hazard?
(a) RAW (b) WAR (c) WAW on R1 (d) none
**Answer: (c).**

**Q24.** 5-stage pipeline without forwarding; registers are written in the first half and read in the second half of a cycle. Stalls between back-to-back dependent ALU instructions?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 3
**Answer: (c).** The producer writes back in cycle 5; the consumer's ID moves from cycle 3 to cycle 5.

**Q25.** 5 stages of 2 ns each, 10 instructions. Total time?
(a) 20 ns (b) 28 ns (c) 100 ns (d) 30 ns
**Answer: (b).** (5 + 9) × 2.

**Q26.** Ideal CPI of a scalar pipeline:
(a) 0.5 (b) 1 (c) k (d) 1/k
**Answer: (b).**

**Q27.** Register renaming eliminates:
(a) RAW hazards (b) WAR and WAW hazards (c) structural hazards (d) control hazards
**Answer: (b).** They are name dependencies, not true data flow.

**Q28.** In a VLIW processor, parallelism is found by:
(a) the hardware at runtime (b) the compiler, which packs independent operations into one long instruction (c) the OS (d) the programmer only
**Answer: (b).**
