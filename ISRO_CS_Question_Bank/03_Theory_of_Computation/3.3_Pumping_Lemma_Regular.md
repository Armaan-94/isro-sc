# 3.3 Pumping Lemma and Identifying Non-Regular Languages

> 24 questions. Quick test: a language is regular if a DFA needs only finite memory. Unbounded counting or comparison means not regular.

---

**Q1.** The pumping lemma for regular languages can prove a language:
(a) is regular (b) is not regular (c) is context-free (d) is decidable
**Answer: (b).** It is a necessary condition only.

**Q2.** In w = xyz:
(a) |y| = 0 allowed (b) |xy| ≤ p and |y| > 0 (c) |yz| ≤ p (d) z empty
**Answer: (b).**

**Q3.** Which IS regular?
(a) {aⁿbⁿ} (b) {aⁿbᵐ : n, m ≥ 0} (c) {ww} (d) {aᵖ : p prime}
**Answer: (b).** It's just a*b*.

**Q4.** In a pumping proof, the split w = xyz is chosen by:
(a) you (b) an adversary; you must handle every valid split (c) uniquely by w (d) irrelevant
**Answer: (b).** You choose w and i.

**Q5.** Common pumping-proof mistake:
(a) |w| < p (b) letting y extend beyond the first p symbols (c) using i = 1 (d) not assuming regularity
**Answer: (b).** Choosing |w| < p is also invalid, but (b) is the classic error.

**Q6.** The standard string for {aⁿbⁿ}:
(a) bᵖbᵖ (b) aᵖbᵖ (c) aᵖ⁺¹bᵖ (d) aᵖbᵖ⁺¹
**Answer: (b).** y must lie inside the a's.

**Q7.** The pumping length p is:
(a) |w| (b) a constant for the language (at most the number of states of a DFA for it) (c) always 1 (d) the number of pumps
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** A language satisfies the pumping conditions. Then it:
(a) is regular (b) may or may not be regular (c) is not regular (d) is finite
**Answer: (b).**

**Q9.** {ww : w ∈ {a, b}*} is not regular because:
(a) even length (b) it needs unbounded memory to compare halves (c) it's regular (d) it contains ε
**Answer: (b).** It isn't even context-free.

**Q10.** To contradict "for all i, xyⁱz ∈ L" you need:
(a) one i where it fails (b) every i failing (c) another string (d) another p
**Answer: (a).** Usually i = 0 or i = 2.

**Q11.** Which is regular?
(a) {aⁿbⁿ : n ≤ 100} (b) {aⁿbⁿ : n ≥ 0} (c) {aⁿ² : n ≥ 0} (d) {aⁿbⁿcⁿ}
**Answer: (a).** Finite, hence regular.

**Q12.** {a²ⁿ : n ≥ 0} is:
(a) regular (b) not regular (c) not context-free (d) undecidable
**Answer: (a).** (aa)*.

**Q13.** {aⁿ² : n ≥ 0} (perfect-square lengths) is:
(a) regular (b) context-free but not regular (c) not context-free (d) finite
**Answer: (c).** Gaps between squares grow without bound; it isn't even a CFL.

**Q14.** {w ∈ {a, b}* : number of a's = number of b's} is:
(a) regular (b) context-free, not regular (c) not context-free (d) finite
**Answer: (b).**

**Q15.** {w : (number of a's) mod 2 = (number of b's) mod 2} is:
(a) regular (b) not regular (c) not context-free (d) undecidable
**Answer: (a).** Only parities are tracked: 4 states.

**Q16.** {aⁿbᵐcᵏ : n, m, k ≥ 0} is:
(a) regular (b) context-free only (c) context-sensitive only (d) not recursively enumerable
**Answer: (a).** a*b*c*.

**Q17.** {0ⁿ1ⁿ : n ≥ 0} ∪ {0ⁿ1ᵐ : n, m ≥ 0} is:
(a) not regular (b) regular (c) not context-free (d) empty
**Answer: (b).** The union is just 0*1*, since the first set is contained in the second.

**Q18.** {aⁿbᵐ : n ≤ m} is:
(a) regular (b) context-free, not regular (c) not context-free (d) finite
**Answer: (b).**

**Q19.** Palindromes over {a, b}:
(a) regular (b) context-free, not regular (c) not context-free (d) finite
**Answer: (b).**

**Q20.** Palindromes over the one-letter alphabet {a}:
(a) regular (b) not regular (c) not context-free (d) empty
**Answer: (a).** Every string over {a} is a palindrome: a*.

**Q21.** If the minimal DFA of L has 5 states, a valid pumping length is:
(a) 1 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) unknown
**Answer: (c).** Any p ≥ number of states works.

**Q22.** By Myhill-Nerode, a language is regular if and only if:
(a) it has finitely many equivalence classes (b) infinitely many (c) it's finite (d) it's context-free
**Answer: (a).**

**Q23.** {aᵖ : p prime} is:
(a) regular (b) context-free, not regular (c) not context-free (d) finite
**Answer: (c).** Over a one-letter alphabet, every CFL is regular, so a non-regular unary language can't be context-free.

**Q24.** Pumping w = aᵖbᵖ at i = 2 gives aᵖ⁺ᵏbᵖ (k ≥ 1), which is:
(a) in L (b) not in L, so a contradiction (c) shorter (d) unchanged
**Answer: (b).**
