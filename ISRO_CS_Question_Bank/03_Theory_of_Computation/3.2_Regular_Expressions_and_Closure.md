# 3.2 Regular Expressions and Closure Properties of Regular Languages

> 26 questions. "+" means union in regular expressions.

---

**Q1.** L* means:
(a) exactly one string of L (b) zero or more concatenations of strings of L (c) one or more (d) complement
**Answer: (b).** Always contains ε.

**Q2.** Regular languages are closed under intersection because:
(a) they aren't (b) of the product construction (or De Morgan with complement and union) (c) only NFAs support it (d) intersection is always empty
**Answer: (b).**

**Q3.** Arden's theorem: R = Q + RP gives:
(a) R = Q + P (b) R = QP* (c) R = P*Q (d) R = (Q + P)*
**Answer: (b).** Provided P doesn't contain ε. (R = Q + PR gives R = P*Q.)

**Q4.** Closure under intersection and complement:
(a) both regular and CFLs closed (b) regular yes; CFLs no (c) CFLs yes; regular no (d) neither
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** Complement of a regular language using a complete DFA:
(a) reverse transitions (b) swap final and non-final states (c) convert to NFA (d) impossible
**Answer: (b).** Doing this on an NFA does NOT give the complement.

**Q6.** R⁺ equals:
(a) R (b) R R* (c) R* R* (d) unrelated
**Answer: (b).**

**Q7.** Regex, NFA, DFA are:
(a) increasing in power (b) equally powerful (c) regex is stronger (d) NFA is stronger
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** Arden's theorem requires:
(a) Q has no ε (b) P has no ε (c) single characters (d) R known
**Answer: (b).**

**Q9.** (a + b)* denotes:
(a) strings of only a's or only b's (b) all strings over {a, b} including ε (c) alternating strings (d) non-empty strings only
**Answer: (b).**

**Q10.** a* + b* vs (a + b)*:
(a) equal (b) a* + b* is a proper subset; it excludes mixed strings like ab (c) (a + b)* is smaller (d) both empty
**Answer: (b).**

**Q11.** Strings over {a, b} containing at least one a:
(a) a* (b) (a + b)* a (a + b)* (c) a⁺b* (d) b* a b*
**Answer: (b).** (d) allows exactly one a.

**Q12.** Even-length strings over {a, b}:
(a) (aa + bb)* (b) ((a + b)(a + b))* (c) (ab)* (d) (a + b)*²
**Answer: (b).** (a) misses ab.

**Q13.** (a + b)* abb describes strings that:
(a) start with abb (b) end with abb (c) contain abb anywhere (d) are exactly abb
**Answer: (b).**

**Q14.** ∅* = ?
(a) ∅ (b) {ε} (c) Σ* (d) undefined
**Answer: (b).** ε* is also {ε}.

**Q15.** (a*)* = ?
(a) a (b) a* (c) a⁺ (d) ε
**Answer: (b).**

**Q16.** Strings of length 3 in (a + b)*?
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 9
**Answer: (c).** 2³.

**Q17.** Strings over {a, b} NOT containing "ab" as a substring:
(a) a*b* (b) b*a* (c) (ba)* (d) (a + b)* − a
**Answer: (b).** Once an a appears, no b may follow.

**Q18.** (a*b*)* equals:
(a) a*b* (b) (a + b)* (c) (ab)* (d) a* + b*
**Answer: (b).**

**Q19.** Which string is NOT in a*b*a*?
(a) aba (b) bab (c) aab (d) baa
**Answer: (b).** After the b-block and a-block, no more b is allowed.

**Q20.** Regular languages are NOT closed under:
(a) union (b) reversal (c) infinite union (d) complement
**Answer: (c).** The union of {aⁿbⁿ} for each n separately gives aⁿbⁿ, not regular.

**Q21.** Intersection of a regular language and a CFL is:
(a) always regular (b) always context-free (c) always undecidable (d) never context-free
**Answer: (b).**

**Q22.** A subset of a regular language:
(a) is always regular (b) need not be regular (c) is never regular (d) is finite
**Answer: (b).** {aⁿbⁿ} ⊆ a*b*.

**Q23.** Regular languages are closed under homomorphism, meaning:
(a) substituting symbols with strings keeps regularity (b) it breaks regularity (c) only finite languages (d) not a closure property
**Answer: (a).**

**Q24.** Difference L₁ − L₂ of regular languages is:
(a) regular (b) context-free only (c) not always regular (d) undecidable
**Answer: (a).** L₁ ∩ complement(L₂).

**Q25.** Strings over {0, 1} ending in 1:
(a) (0 + 1)*1 (b) 1(0 + 1)* (c) 0*1 (d) 1*
**Answer: (a).**

**Q26.** The language of a regular expression containing only concatenations and unions (no star) is:
(a) infinite (b) finite (c) empty (d) non-regular
**Answer: (b).** Infinite languages need a star.
