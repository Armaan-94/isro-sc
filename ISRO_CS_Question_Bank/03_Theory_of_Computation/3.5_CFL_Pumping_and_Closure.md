# 3.5 Pumping Lemma for CFLs and Closure Properties of CFLs

> 24 questions. Rule of thumb: one stack can match one pair of counts (or nested pairs), never three linked counts or crossed pairs.

---

**Q1.** In w = uvxyz (CFL pumping lemma), v and y:
(a) only v non-empty (b) are pumped together the same number of times; at least one is non-empty (c) only y non-empty (d) both must be non-empty
**Answer: (b).** Also |vxy| ≤ p.

**Q2.** Context-free despite involving counting:
(a) {aⁿbⁿcⁿ} (b) {aⁿbⁿ} (c) {ww} (d) {aⁱbʲcᵏ : i < j < k}
**Answer: (b).**

**Q3.** CFLs are closed under:
(a) intersection (b) complement (c) union, concatenation, star (d) difference
**Answer: (c).**

**Q4.** CFL ∩ regular is:
(a) never CFL (b) always CFL (c) CFL only if finite (d) always regular
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** {aⁿbⁿcᵐ} ∩ {aᵐbⁿcⁿ} = ?
(a) {aⁿbⁿcⁿ}, not a CFL (b) ∅ (c) {abc} (d) all strings
**Answer: (a).** Proof that CFLs aren't closed under intersection.

**Q6.** {aⁱbʲcᵏ : i < j < k} is not a CFL because:
(a) it's regular (b) it links three unbounded counts (c) it's finite (d) it's ambiguous
**Answer: (b).**

**Q7.** The CFL pumping length relates to:
(a) alphabet size (b) the number of variables in a CNF grammar for L (c) terminals only (d) nothing
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** Satisfying the CFL pumping conditions:
(a) proves context-free (b) is necessary only; used to disprove (c) proves regular (d) useless
**Answer: (b).**

**Q9.** CFLs closed under reversal means:
(a) reversing every string of a CFL gives a CFL (b) reversal makes it regular (c) only regular (d) undefined
**Answer: (a).**

**Q10.** A PDA can't recognise {aⁿbⁿcⁿ} because:
(a) too few states (b) one stack can enforce only one count match (c) it can't read c (d) alphabet too big
**Answer: (b).**

**Q11.** Which is a CFL?
(a) {aⁿbᵐcᵐ : n, m ≥ 0} (b) {aⁿbⁿcⁿ} (c) {aⁿbᵐcⁿdᵐ} (d) {ww}
**Answer: (a).** One independent block plus one matched pair.

**Q12.** {aⁿbᵐcᵐdⁿ : n, m ≥ 0} is:
(a) regular (b) CFL (nested matching) (c) not CFL (d) finite
**Answer: (b).** Nested pairs fit a stack.

**Q13.** {aⁿbᵐcⁿdᵐ : n, m ≥ 0} is:
(a) regular (b) CFL (c) not CFL (crossed matching) (d) finite
**Answer: (c).**

**Q14.** {aⁱbʲcᵏ : i = j or j = k} is:
(a) not CFL (b) CFL, and inherently ambiguous (c) regular (d) DCFL
**Answer: (b).** A union of two CFLs. With "and" instead of "or" it becomes aⁿbⁿcⁿ, not a CFL.

**Q15.** {ww^R : w ∈ {a, b}*} is:
(a) regular (b) CFL but not DCFL (c) not CFL (d) DCFL
**Answer: (b).**

**Q16.** For CFLs, which problem is decidable?
(a) equivalence of two CFGs (b) ambiguity of a CFG (c) emptiness of L(G) (d) whether L(G) = Σ*
**Answer: (c).** Membership and finiteness are also decidable.

**Q17.** The complement of a CFL is:
(a) always CFL (b) not necessarily CFL (c) always regular (d) never CFL
**Answer: (b).** It is always recursive, though.

**Q18.** The union of two DCFLs is:
(a) always a DCFL (b) a CFL, but not necessarily a DCFL (c) regular (d) not a CFL
**Answer: (b).**

**Q19.** CFLs are closed under Kleene star. If L = {aⁿbⁿ}, then L* is:
(a) regular (b) a CFL (c) not a CFL (d) finite
**Answer: (b).**

**Q20.** The difference of two CFLs is:
(a) always a CFL (b) not necessarily a CFL (c) regular (d) empty
**Answer: (b).** L₁ − L₂ involves complement and intersection.

**Q21.** CFL − regular (L₁ − R) is:
(a) always a CFL (b) not necessarily a CFL (c) regular (d) undecidable
**Answer: (a).** L₁ ∩ complement(R), and complement(R) is regular.

**Q22.** {aⁿ : n ≥ 0} ∪ {aⁿbⁿ : n ≥ 0} is:
(a) regular (b) CFL (c) not CFL (d) finite
**Answer: (b).** A union of a regular language and a CFL; still contains the non-regular aⁿbⁿ part.

**Q23.** {aⁿbⁿ : n ≥ 0} ∩ {a*b*} is:
(a) {aⁿbⁿ} (b) a*b* (c) ∅ (d) regular
**Answer: (a).**

**Q24.** A language over a single letter that is context-free must be:
(a) regular (b) finite (c) undecidable (d) inherently ambiguous
**Answer: (a).** Parikh's theorem consequence.
