# 7.2 Data Communication Fundamentals

**Notes:** [02 Data Communication Fundamentals](../../CN_Cybersecurity_ISRO_Notes/02_Data_Communication_Fundamentals.md)

> 24 questions. Nyquist (noiseless): 2B log₂ L. Shannon (noisy): B log₂(1 + SNR). SNR(dB) = 10 log₁₀ SNR.

---

**Q1.** Frequency 500 Hz. Period?
(a) 500 s (b) 2 ms (c) 0.5 ms (d) 5 ms
**Answer: (b).**

**Q2.** 4 bits per symbol at 2400 baud. Bit rate?
(a) 600 (b) 2400 (c) 4800 (d) 9600 bps
**Answer: (d).**

**Q3.** Link rated 100 Mbps delivers 40 Mbps. The 40 Mbps is:
(a) bandwidth (b) baud rate (c) throughput (d) capacity
**Answer: (c).**

**Q4.** Noiseless, 3000 Hz, 2 levels. Maximum bit rate?
(a) 3000 (b) 6000 (c) 1500 (d) 9000 bps
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** 3 kHz, SNR 7. Shannon capacity?
(a) 3000 (b) 6000 (c) 9000 (d) 12000 bps
**Answer: (c).**

**Q6.** Nyquist vs Shannon:
(a) Nyquist noisy (b) Nyquist uses SNR (c) Nyquist: noiseless, signal levels; Shannon: noisy, SNR (d) identical
**Answer: (c).**

**Q7.** Manchester is least efficient because:
(a) no error detection (b) two transitions per bit for synchronisation (c) more levels (d) analog only
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** Base64 turns 3 bytes into:
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6 characters
**Answer: (c).**

**Q9.** Noiseless, B = 4000 Hz, L = 4. Maximum bit rate?
(a) 8 kbps (b) 16 kbps (c) 32 kbps (d) 4 kbps
**Answer: (b).** 2 × 4000 × 2.

**Q10.** B = 1 MHz, SNR = 63. Capacity?
(a) 1 Mbps (b) 6 Mbps (c) 63 Mbps (d) 64 Mbps
**Answer: (b).** log₂ 64 = 6.

**Q11.** SNR of 30 dB as a ratio?
(a) 30 (b) 100 (c) 1000 (d) 3
**Answer: (c).**

**Q12.** B = 3000 Hz, SNR = 30 dB. Capacity approximately?
(a) 9 kbps (b) 30 kbps (c) 90 kbps (d) 3 kbps
**Answer: (b).** 3000 × log₂ 1001 ≈ 3000 × 9.97.

**Q13.** Noiseless 3000 Hz channel must carry 12,000 bps. Signal levels needed?
(a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 16
**Answer: (b).** 2 × 3000 × log₂ L = 12000 → log₂ L = 2.

**Q14.** A 1000-bit frame on a 1 Mbps link. Transmission delay?
(a) 1 µs (b) 1 ms (c) 1 s (d) 10 ms
**Answer: (b).**

**Q15.** 2000 km at 2 × 10⁸ m/s. Propagation delay?
(a) 1 ms (b) 10 ms (c) 100 ms (d) 0.1 ms
**Answer: (b).**

**Q16.** 10 Mbps link sends 12,000 frames/min of 10,000 bits each. Efficiency?
(a) 10% (b) 20% (c) 50% (d) 100%
**Answer: (b).** 2 Mbps / 10 Mbps.

**Q17.** 10 Mbps Manchester-encoded Ethernet. Baud rate?
(a) 5 Mbaud (b) 10 Mbaud (c) 20 Mbaud (d) 40 Mbaud
**Answer: (c).** Two signal elements per bit.

**Q18.** Voice band-limited to 4 kHz, sampled at the Nyquist rate with 8 bits per sample. PCM bit rate?
(a) 32 kbps (b) 64 kbps (c) 8 kbps (d) 128 kbps
**Answer: (b).** 8000 samples/s × 8.

**Q19.** PCM steps in order:
(a) sampling, quantisation, encoding (b) encoding, sampling, quantisation (c) quantisation, sampling, encoding (d) modulation only
**Answer: (a).**

**Q20.** QAM combines:
(a) amplitude and phase shift keying (b) frequency and time (c) codes (d) Manchester and NRZ
**Answer: (a).**

**Q21.** Higher-order modulation (more bits per symbol) needs:
(a) a cleaner, less noisy channel (b) more noise (c) lower bandwidth always (d) no synchronisation
**Answer: (a).**

**Q22.** Throughput compared to bandwidth is always:
(a) greater (b) less than or equal (c) equal (d) unrelated
**Answer: (b).**

**Q23.** Digital signals vs analog in noise:
(a) digital can be regenerated, more noise-immune (b) analog more immune (c) equal (d) digital can't travel far
**Answer: (a).**

**Q24.** Total latency includes:
(a) transmission, propagation, queuing and processing delays (b) only propagation (c) only transmission (d) bandwidth
**Answer: (a).**
