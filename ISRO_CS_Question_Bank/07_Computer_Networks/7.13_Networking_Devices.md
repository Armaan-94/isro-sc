# 7.13 Networking Hardware Devices

> 20 questions. Hubs separate nothing; switches/bridges separate collision domains; routers separate broadcast domains.

---

**Q1.** Purely physical layer, no MAC awareness:
(a) bridge (b) switch (c) hub (d) router
**Answer: (c).**

**Q2.** Separates collision but not broadcast domains:
(a) router (b) hub (c) switch (d) repeater
**Answer: (c).**

**Q3.** Connects networks with different protocols (protocol conversion):
(a) repeater (b) hub (c) gateway (d) bridge
**Answer: (c).**

**Q4.** Switch over hub because:
(a) cheaper (b) each port is its own collision domain (c) physical layer only (d) no full duplex
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** Forwards using IP addresses and routing tables:
(a) switch (b) bridge (c) router (d) repeater
**Answer: (c).**

**Q6.** A hub with 8 PCs. Collision and broadcast domains?
(a) 1 and 1 (b) 8 and 1 (c) 8 and 8 (d) 1 and 8
**Answer: (a).**

**Q7.** A switch with 8 PCs (no VLANs). Collision and broadcast domains?
(a) 1, 1 (b) 8, 1 (c) 8, 8 (d) 1, 8
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** Two hubs with 4 PCs each, joined by a bridge. Collision and broadcast domains?
(a) 1, 1 (b) 2, 1 (c) 8, 1 (d) 2, 2
**Answer: (b).**

**Q9.** A router with 3 interfaces, each to a switch with 5 PCs. Broadcast domains?
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 15
**Answer: (b).**

**Q10.** Same network: collision domains (each switch port, including the uplink to the router)?
(a) 3 (b) 15 (c) 18 (d) 1
**Answer: (c).** 6 per switch × 3.

**Q11.** VLANs on a switch:
(a) split one switch into several broadcast domains (b) merge collision domains (c) replace routers for routing between VLANs (d) remove MAC tables
**Answer: (a).** Inter-VLAN traffic still needs layer 3.

**Q12.** A repeater differs from an amplifier because it:
(a) regenerates the digital signal, not amplifying noise (b) amplifies noise (c) routes packets (d) filters frames
**Answer: (a).**

**Q13.** A switch receiving a frame for an unknown destination MAC:
(a) drops it (b) floods it out all ports except the incoming one (c) sends to the router (d) sends an ARP
**Answer: (b).**

**Q14.** A switch learns MAC addresses from:
(a) the source MAC of incoming frames (b) destination MACs (c) DNS (d) the router
**Answer: (a).**

**Q15.** A modem's job:
(a) modulate and demodulate between digital data and analog signals (b) routing (c) switching (d) encryption
**Answer: (a).**

**Q16.** The NIC holds the device's:
(a) MAC address (b) IP address permanently (c) port number (d) domain name
**Answer: (a).**

**Q17.** A bridge connects:
(a) LAN segments of the same technology, filtering by MAC (b) different protocols (c) WANs by IP (d) only two PCs
**Answer: (a).**

**Q18.** Broadcast traffic passes through:
(a) hubs, bridges and switches, but not routers (b) routers only (c) nothing (d) only hubs
**Answer: (a).**

**Q19.** A wireless access point mainly works at:
(a) layer 1 and 2 (b) layer 3 only (c) layer 7 (d) layer 4
**Answer: (a).** It bridges wireless clients to the wired LAN.

**Q20.** A layer-3 switch:
(a) combines switching with IP routing in hardware (b) is a hub (c) is a repeater (d) works only at layer 1
**Answer: (a).**
