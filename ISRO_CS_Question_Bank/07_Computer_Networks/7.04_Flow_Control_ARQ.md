# 7.4 Data Link Layer: Flow Control (Stop-and-Wait, Go-Back-N, Selective Repeat)

> 26 questions. a = Tp/Tt. Stop-and-wait efficiency = 1/(1 + 2a). Window N: efficiency = min(1, N/(1 + 2a)). GBN window ≤ 2ᵐ − 1; SR window ≤ 2ᵐ⁻¹.

---

**Q1.** Tt = 1 ms, Tp = 4 ms. Stop-and-wait efficiency?
(a) 20% (b) 11.1% (c) 25% (d) 50%
**Answer: (b).** 1/9.

**Q2.** Same link: window for 100% utilisation?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 9 (d) 5
**Answer: (c).** 1 + 2a.

**Q3.** m = 3: maximum GBN and SR sender windows?
(a) 8, 4 (b) 7, 4 (c) 8, 8 (d) 7, 8
**Answer: (b).**

**Q4.** SR's window is capped at 2ᵐ⁻¹ because:
(a) timers (b) otherwise old retransmissions could be mistaken for new frames (c) flow control (d) no sequence numbers
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** GBN sent 4, 5, 6, 7; frame 4 times out. Resend:
(a) 4 (b) 4, 5, 6, 7 (c) 7 (d) nothing
**Answer: (b).**

**Q6.** Receiver needs a reordering buffer in:
(a) stop-and-wait (b) GBN (c) selective repeat (d) all
**Answer: (c).**

**Q7.** Stop-and-wait needs only 1 sequence bit because:
(a) no errors (b) only one frame is ever outstanding (c) ACKs are numbered (d) window 2ᵐ − 1
**Answer: (b).**

**Q8.** Tt = 1 ms, Tp = 4 ms, GBN window 7. Efficiency?
(a) 77.8% (b) 100% (c) 11.1% (d) 70%
**Answer: (a).** 7/9.

**Q9.** 1000-bit frames, 1 Mbps, 2000 km, 2 × 10⁸ m/s. a = ?
(a) 1 (b) 5 (c) 10 (d) 20
**Answer: (c).** Tt = 1 ms, Tp = 10 ms.

**Q10.** Same link, stop-and-wait efficiency?
(a) 9.1% (b) about 4.76% (c) 10% (d) 50%
**Answer: (b).** 1/21.

**Q11.** Same link, stop-and-wait throughput?
(a) 1 Mbps (b) about 47.6 kbps (c) 100 kbps (d) 10 kbps
**Answer: (b).**

**Q12.** Same link: minimum sequence bits for full utilisation with GBN?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 21
**Answer: (b).** Need window 21 ≤ 2ᵐ − 1 → m = 5.

**Q13.** Same link with selective repeat?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 7
**Answer: (c).** Need 2ᵐ⁻¹ ≥ 21 → m = 6.

**Q14.** GBN window 7; frames 0–6 sent; frame 3 lost (others arrive). Frames retransmitted?
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 7
**Answer: (c).** 3, 4, 5, 6.

**Q15.** Same situation under selective repeat?
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 7
**Answer: (a).** Only frame 3.

**Q16.** Bandwidth 1 Mbps, RTT 20 ms. Bandwidth-delay product?
(a) 20 kbits (b) 2 kbits (c) 200 kbits (d) 20 Mbits
**Answer: (a).** Bits that can be "in flight".

**Q17.** GBN receiver window size:
(a) 1 (b) 2ᵐ − 1 (c) 2ᵐ⁻¹ (d) unlimited
**Answer: (a).**

**Q18.** GBN uses:
(a) cumulative ACKs and one timer (for the oldest frame) (b) individual ACKs and per-frame timers (c) NAKs only (d) no ACKs
**Answer: (a).**

**Q19.** Selective repeat needs:
(a) per-frame timers and individual ACKs/NAKs (b) one timer (c) no buffer (d) receiver window 1
**Answer: (a).**

**Q20.** Best on noisy links with large a:
(a) stop-and-wait (b) GBN (c) selective repeat (d) all equal
**Answer: (c).**

**Q21.** Flow control prevents:
(a) a fast sender overwhelming a slow receiver (b) bit errors (c) routing loops (d) congestion in routers only
**Answer: (a).**

**Q22.** With a ≤ 0.5 (short link), stop-and-wait efficiency is at least:
(a) 50% (b) 25% (c) 10% (d) 90%
**Answer: (a).** 1/(1 + 1).

**Q23.** In stop-and-wait, a corrupted frame is:
(a) discarded silently; the sender's timer triggers retransmission (b) corrected (c) NAKed always (d) delivered
**Answer: (a).**

**Q24.** m = 4: maximum SR window?
(a) 8 (b) 15 (c) 16 (d) 4
**Answer: (a).** GBN would be 15.

**Q25.** Stop-and-wait cycle time:
(a) Tt + 2Tp (b) Tt (c) 2Tt (d) Tp
**Answer: (a).**

**Q26.** Efficiency of stop-and-wait drops as:
(a) distance and bandwidth increase (larger a) (b) frames get bigger (c) a decreases (d) never
**Answer: (a).**
